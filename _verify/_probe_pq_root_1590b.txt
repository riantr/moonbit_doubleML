WHAT I FIRST DID (WRONG): kept the IPW bisection as theta_prelim, cross-fit g
at it, then BISECTED the orthogonal score over the full-width bracket
[y_min - margin, y_max + margin] and used a central difference for deriv.

WHY THE BISECTION IS WRONG FOR THIS SCORE: 1{y <= theta} is a STEP function, so
mean(score) is piecewise CONSTANT in theta. A bisection converges onto a jump,
and the midpoint of a jump is not a root. Measured (v0.139.0 quantile-sweep
fixture, q=0.9/0.1 arms):
    q=0.9 treated arm: theta_prelim=1.790 theta=3.005 mean(psi)=-0.161
    q=0.1 control arm: theta_prelim=-0.588 theta=-2.070 mean(psi)=+0.034
                                ^^^^^^ BELOW min(y) entirely (~-1.4)

WHY THE CENTRAL-DIFFERENCE DERIV IS WRONG: it differences the step function, so
it is IDENTICALLY ZERO whenever no observation lies within +/- h of theta --
which is exactly the out-of-support case above. Measured deriv = 0 at q=0.1.
DoubleMLQTE::fit scales psi by 1/deriv with no guard (quantile.mbt:2411-2415
documents this), so 0/0 = NaN reached ses[j]:
    OLD ses = [NaN, 0.3019, 0.1758, 0.1994, 1.6937]
That is a REAL defect, caught by the existing gate
v1390_qte_multi_quantile_fit_returns_distinct_estimates (assert se > 0.0).
It was NOT a stale pin and was deliberately not re-pinned.

THE CORRECTED FIX:
  * Newton from theta_prelim against the orthogonal score (upstream's solver).
  * deriv = gaussian_kde_weighted(data.y, ind_d/m, theta,
                                  silverman_bandwidth(data.y)) / n
    i.e. upstream's _compute_score_deriv / _default_kde, reusing the KDE
    helpers that already existed in this repo (kde.mbt:14, kde.mbt:125).
    A Gaussian never evaluates to 0, so the NaN SE is structurally unreachable.
  * this also drops the two extra g cross-fits per quantile that the central
    difference needed.
