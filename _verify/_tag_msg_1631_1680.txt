0.163.1 -- LPLR reads its own nuisances the way upstream does, and the
gates that could not fail now can

Four upstream inputs the LPLR coefficient and standard error were computed
from were wrong. `W_inner` was built from ml_M's OUTER cross-fit where
upstream reads the inner one (plm/lplr.py:318), `beta` was one averaged
scalar where upstream keeps it per observation (:315-323, :345), the reported
coefficient was never the root at all (upstream takes root_res.root), and the
Newton started from an ad-hoc OLS scale instead of np.average(beta) (:326).

On the 200-design sweep: designs exceeding 1e4 go 88/200 -> 0, the worst
absolute coefficient 1.03e11 -> 24.18, the median 6.35 -> 1.89. The gated
n=500 numbers barely move -- 0.007307 / 0.010609 -- which is the signature of
a fix that only bites where the solver was actually failing.

Gate work: one comparison compared model.warnings() to a second call on the
same object and so could not fail; one alpha-suffix invariant swept a range
that returned all zeros; two CI-identity tolerances asserted luck rather than
arithmetic (one was 0.91 ulp on wasm-gc). All closed and mutation-verified.

KNOWN DIVERGENCE, REPORTED NOT GATED: at n=500 the port and upstream agree to
~0.007, and that is the one design point this estimator was never wrong at.
Below it, 91 of 200 small-n designs still warn where upstream warns zero
times. The cause is pinned to the Newton step -- the port damps it and scipy
does not -- and the damping stays because removing it reaches se = NaN by
trial 2 of examples/fuzz while upstream is 0/300 non-finite on the same DGP.
Two candidate causes were implemented and measured and both are falsified.
See README.mbt.md and _verify/LPLR_GATE_BLINDNESS_1680.md sections 11-16.

Tests 1117 / 1123 / 1117 / 1117 (native / wasm-gc / wasm / js), unchanged.
